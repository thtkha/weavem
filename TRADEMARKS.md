# Weavem trademark policy

Weavem™ is the project name and trademark claimed by Kha Trịnh. This policy does
not assert that the mark is registered. It describes use of the Weavem name as a
brand, separately from the license for the source code.

Our principle is:

> If you keep it proprietary, use your own brand. If you want to call it Weavem,
> contribute back to the commons.

## Code and branding are separate

The code is available under the [Apache License 2.0](LICENSE). Subject to that
license, you may use, modify, distribute, sell, and host the code, including as part
of proprietary products and commercial services under your own brand. Those uses
do not require trademark permission or publication of your proprietary source.

This policy does not modify the Apache License or add source-disclosure conditions
to its code permissions. Apache's trademark provision is in Section 6; its attribution
requirements remain applicable independently of this policy. See also [NOTICE](NOTICE).

For example, a proprietary service called **Acme Data Cloud**, described truthfully
as **built with Weavem**, is different from naming that service **Weavem Cloud**.

## Descriptive use and attribution

You may truthfully describe your software as using, built with, based on, or compatible
with Weavem. You may identify the origin of the code, preserve attribution notices,
and discuss or review the project. Make your own product's identity clear.

Such references must not misleadingly suggest that your product or service is
produced, operated, sponsored, certified, or endorsed by the Weavem project.
Retaining the project's name in required notices is not permission to adopt it as
your product's brand, and using your own brand is not permission to remove required
attribution.

Nothing in this policy restricts descriptive, nominative, or other uses permitted
by applicable law. Ordinary references to the original Weavem project or its releases
are not a request to brand a new product.

## Product and service names

Except for uses permitted by applicable law, using Weavem as the primary name or
branding of another product, hosted service, software distribution, or derivative
project requires **prior written permission** from Kha Trịnh. This applies to both
commercial and noncommercial offerings, including forks.

Names such as **Weavem Cloud**, **Weavem Enterprise**, and **Weavem Platform** require
that permission when used to brand your offering. Do not imply official project
status or endorsement without express written authorization. A fork without separate
branding permission should use its own name while preserving required attribution.

## Open-source condition for branded derivatives

Permission to use Weavem as the primary brand of a derivative product or service
requires a separate written trademark agreement. Such permission is conditioned
on making the Weavem-derived implementation publicly available under an open-source
license approved in that agreement.

Here, **contribute back** means making usable source available to the public. It does
not require that an upstream pull request be accepted, and submitting a patch alone
does not satisfy the condition. Publishing source does **not** automatically grant
trademark permission: approval is still required.

Before branded use begins, the agreement must identify:

- The approved product or service, operator, names, and permitted presentation.
- The covered Weavem-derived code and modifications, including the implementation
  used to operate a branded hosted service—not merely an unrelated public repository.
- An OSI-approved open-source license agreed by the parties.
- The public source location, required build instructions, and publication timing.
- How published source will stay aligned with branded releases and service updates
  throughout the permission period.
- Attribution, quality and no-confusion requirements, the duration of permission,
  and what happens if the conditions are breached or permission ends.

The source-publication scope must be agreed explicitly. Customer data, credentials,
secrets, and unrelated independent systems are not source to disclose under this
policy. If the required covered implementation cannot be published, use your own
brand instead. This policy itself is not a signed permission agreement.

Wait for express written approval before adopting the brand. Silence, a contribution,
or approval of a code change is not trademark permission.

## Legal scope

This is the project's branding policy, not a claim of rights beyond applicable law
or a guarantee of trademark registration or enforceability in every jurisdiction.
The project owner should obtain trademark counsel's review before relying on it or
a permission agreement commercially, including review of ownership, clearance,
quality control, and the agreement's source-publication obligations.
